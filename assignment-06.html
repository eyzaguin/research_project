A0612-assignment project guide

Static visualization

Turning analysis into effective visual communication.

Research question

Make the evidence clear enough to stand on its own.

These five figures redesign the most important findings from Assignment 05 for the research paper: the popularity distribution, genre differences, pooled correlations, and the relationships between popularity and danceability or energy.

Each pair shows the exploratory figure first and its redesigned counterpart second. The redesigns use direct labels, consistent scales, clearer visual hierarchy, high-contrast colors that do not rely on color alone, and annotations that explain the evidence without implying causation.

Original → redesigned · five research figures

From exploratory plots to paper-ready evidence.

All redesigned charts are regenerated from the prepared 113,550-row Spotify file. Read the title, scale, sample notes, annotations, and alt text together; each chart reports descriptive patterns in this dataset, not causal effects or Spotify-wide estimates.

01

Finding 01 · Distribution

A large zero-score group shapes the popularity distribution.

Median popularity is 35. A total of 15,844 tracks (14.0%) score zero; only 5,468 (4.8%) score 70 or higher.

Research connection: The zero pile-up and right tail mean the study should report medians and distribution shape alongside averages when describing popularity.

02

Finding 02 · Group differences

Mean popularity differs by 57.1 points across the selected genre extremes.

Among the ten lowest- and ten highest-mean genres, Iranian averages 2.2 and pop-film averages 59.3. Genre groups contain 904–1,000 rows.

Research connection: Genre is a major comparison and possible confounder in audio-feature analyses. These source groups are nearly balanced by design and tracks can have multiple genre labels, so the difference is descriptive—not a measure of genre effects on Spotify listening.

03

Finding 03 · Correlations

The pooled linear correlations between popularity and these audio features are near zero.

Across seven features, Pearson r ranges from −0.045 for speechiness to 0.034 for danceability.

Research connection: No single measured audio feature has a meaningful pooled linear association with popularity here. This motivates multivariable and subgroup analysis; correlation does not establish causality or rule out nonlinear patterns.

04

Finding 04 · Relationship

Popularity rises modestly across middle danceability bands, then levels off.

Binned mean popularity peaks at 34.7 for danceability 0.5–0.6 and is 31.4 for 0.9–1.0; pooled Pearson r is 0.034.

Research connection: The shallow, non-monotonic pattern cautions against claiming that greater danceability predicts greater popularity. Examine genre-specific relationships before drawing broader conclusions.

05

Finding 05 · Relationship

Mean popularity is highest at moderate energy bands, not at the most energetic end.

Mean popularity peaks at 36.1 for energy 0.4–0.5 and falls to 30.9 for 0.9–1.0; pooled Pearson r is −0.002.

Research connection: The overall correlation is effectively zero even though the binned averages are non-monotonic. A linear coefficient alone misses this shape; the chart supports testing nonlinear and genre-specific patterns without claiming energy causes popularity.

Research paper · figure use

A clear figure is not a causal result.

These redesigned visuals are candidates for the paper's primary descriptive figures. They are computed from the prepared dataset, which has nearly equal numbers of rows per genre and includes tracks assigned to multiple genres. Spotify popularity is not explained by sound alone, and this dataset contains no release-year or geographic field—so time-series and geographic charts would imply evidence this project does not have. Describe associations only, retain the source and sample limitations, and pair each chart with a concise finding in the Results section.
